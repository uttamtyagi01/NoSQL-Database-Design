Q2. Compare Apache HBase and Apache Cassandra.

HBase uses a Bigtable-style column-family model with row keys, column families, qualifiers, cells and versions. Cassandra uses a partitioned wide-column model with tables, partition keys and clustering columns.

HBase commonly uses HMaster, RegionServers, ZooKeeper and HDFS. Cassandra uses a peer-to-peer architecture without a traditional master.

HBase generally provides strong consistency for its normal read/write model. Cassandra provides tunable consistency through levels such as ONE, QUORUM and ALL.

Both provide horizontal scalability and fault tolerance. HBase distributes regions among RegionServers and uses HDFS replication. Cassandra distributes partitions across nodes and uses configurable replication.

HBase operations include Get, Put, Scan and Delete. Cassandra uses CQL for INSERT, SELECT, UPDATE and DELETE.

HBase is suitable for very large datasets integrated with the Hadoop ecosystem. Cassandra is particularly suitable for highly distributed, always-on and geographically distributed applications.

For a highly distributed application, Cassandra would generally be preferred because of its peer-to-peer architecture, replication, tunable consistency and high availability.
