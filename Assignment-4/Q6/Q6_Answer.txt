Q6. Discuss consistency and transactions in HBase and Cassandra.

Traditional RDBMSs emphasize ACID transactions, while distributed NoSQL systems make trade-offs because data is spread across multiple machines and networks can fail.

HBase provides strong consistency for its normal read/write model and supports atomic operations at the row level, but it is not a general relational-style distributed transaction system across arbitrary rows.

Cassandra provides tunable consistency using levels such as ONE, QUORUM and ALL. It also supports lightweight transactions for specific conditional operations.

Distributed databases trade off consistency, latency and availability because replicas can be temporarily different and network failures can occur.

Application design must select appropriate consistency for each operation. Critical data may need stronger consistency, while analytics or activity feeds may tolerate weaker/tunable consistency. Data should also be modeled to minimize the need for multi-row distributed transactions.
